# Mechanism and sensitivity analysis

27 September 2026. The analyses below were specified after the final-evaluation outcomes were observed. They are exploratory; the primary comparison, thresholds and prespecified sensitivity classifications remain fixed.

## Diagnostic scope

1. **Trading frequency and size.** Decompose annual routine turnover exactly into 252 times the active-day fraction times average turnover on active days. Use the saved positive routine-turnover amounts, with no new economic dust cutoff. Separately report actual costs/turnover by trade reason and decision-trigger categories. Reinvestment and cap repair remain included.
2. **Alternative comparators.** Retain the frozen primary A/B pair. Show A's point risk/turnover ratios against every other original grid policy in original order. Give exploratory paired intervals for the three calendar MV rules and the two previously selected secondary comparisons, reusing the same bootstrap settings. Do not select a replacement comparator or claim multiplicity-adjusted discovery across these alternatives.
3. **Market episodes and initialization.** Attribute the full-sample annual variance difference additively by calendar year using full-sample means and N-1 denominator. Show the fraction of total squared gross returns accounted for by the largest 1 and 10 absolute-return sessions, and turnover attributable to the largest 1 and 10 trading sessions. Report A/B point ratios after excluding the first 21 and 63 recorded sessions from the continuous ledger. These are descriptive sensitivity slices, not new self-financing policy paths or independent confirmatory tests. Reuse the fixed-year, two-year and leave-one-year-out checks from the final evaluation.
4. **Concentration and equity exposure.** Report time-average closing ETF sleeve weights, Herfindahl concentration and effective asset count for A, B, daily/weekly/monthly MV, monthly equal weight and the frozen secondary comparators. Use an intercept OLS projection of each gross return series on the monthly equal-weight reference solely as descriptive exposure accounting; verify variance = beta-squared times reference variance plus residual variance. Do not call the intercept alpha or this a causal adjustment.
5. **Decisions from common portfolio states.** At the last observed session of each test calendar month (72 dates), use A's actual current holdings and the original dated 252-day covariance. Recompute A, penalty 0.3, band 0.08 and the full-MV target at that identical state. Record turnover and forecast risk together; the policies need not target identical risk. Verify the replayed A against its recorded action and objective. Save a two-dimensional allocation-plane illustration of the already-prespecified first partial rebalance, overlaying the feasible risk region and the selected weight band. This is mechanism description, not a tradable synthetic history.
6. **Future-data perturbation checks.** Replay A, B and monthly MV on two historical input windows: 2020-03-02 to 2020-04-15 with cutoff 2020-03-16; and 2025-11-24 to 2025-12-19 with cutoff 2025-12-04. Each diagnostic window starts from the common reset state. In a second run, perturb only post-cutoff opens/closes, risk observations and future cash-distribution amounts. Earlier daily ledgers and decision contents must agree, disregarding only terminal/execution status metadata that may depend on later dates. Confirm the post-cutoff paths actually differ. These are leakage checks, not performance estimates. Existing synthetic future-perturbation tests also remain required.

## Existing predeclared checks and interpretation

Reuse all nine frozen scenarios, the baseline bootstrap lengths 10/20/60, target/solver diagnostics and the deterministic examples. The 126-day interval misses the turnover ceiling; 2020 alone misses the point turnover target. Diagonal covariance changes both targets and triggers, so its result cannot identify a pure causal contribution of off-diagonal risk geometry. The exploratory order-queue diagnostic already shows that delayed-execution savings depend strongly on stale pending targets; no new queue policy is introduced here.

## Reproducibility

The review uses the original policies, ledger, inference implementation, 115 financial input files, selected comparator and final-evaluation results. Source and input hashes accompany the diagnostic tables. Numerical or accounting mismatches must be resolved before interpreting a comparison. Results report both supporting evidence and failures of the proposed explanation.
